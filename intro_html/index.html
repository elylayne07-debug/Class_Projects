Automotive Repair

What is Automotive Repair?

Automotive repair is the work involved in diagnosing, maintaining, and fixing problems in cars and trucks so they remain safe, reliable, and efficient on the road. It includes tasks like inspecting mechanical and electrical systems, replacing worn out parts, performing routine maintenance such as oil changes and brake service, and solving issues that affect performance or safety. Automotive repair combines technical skill, specialized tools, and knowledge of vehicle systems to keep vehicles running properly and to prevent small issues from becoming major, costly problems.

What education do you need?

To become an automotive repair technician, you usually start with a high school diploma or GED. Most people then attend a trade school or community college to complete a one or two year automotive technology program. These programs teach essential skills like engine repair, diagnostics, electrical systems, and safety procedures. Many employers also prefer certifications such as ASE to show mastery in specific repair areas. After training, hands on experience through entry level work or apprenticeships helps build the practical skills needed for the job.
